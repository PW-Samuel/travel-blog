I’ve been traveling since 2017, chasing moments instead of plans. I’m fascinated by snakes, love sharks, would happily spend weeks deep in the jungle, and can never get enough papaya. As long as there’s a next destination, I know I’m exactly where I’m meant to be.

TOP 5 beach destinations for 2026

Choosing the best beaches of 2026 was anything but easy. After years of traveling, I've learned that a perfect beach is about much more than soft sand and clear water. It's the moment you arrive, kick off your shoes and feel your whole body slow down. Every place on this list gave me exactly that feeling.

You'll find snorkeling spots where colorful fish swim right up to your fingertips, quiet stretches of sand where the only sound is the rolling surf, and golden-hour sunsets that are worth planning an entire trip around. Some of these beaches are easy to reach, others take a boat ride, a bumpy road or a little patience, but I promise the effort is always rewarded.

Before you book your trip, keep these essentials in mind:

1. Travel in the shoulder season. You'll get calmer beaches, lower prices and still enough sunshine to enjoy every day.
2. Check the water conditions before you swim. Currents, jellyfish and tides can change quickly, so ask locals for advice.
3. Pack reef-safe sunscreen. It protects your skin and the marine life you came to see.
4. Bring cash and water. Remote beaches rarely have shops, and many small vendors don't accept cards.

I arrived late in the afternoon, just as the sun was sinking toward the horizon. The small wooden bungalow I had booked stood only a few steps from the water, and from the porch I could already hear the waves rolling onto the sand. I dropped my bag, kicked off my shoes and walked straight to the shore. That first evening, with warm water around my ankles and the sky turning orange and pink, I knew this week would be special. My days quickly found a gentle rhythm. I woke up at sunrise, when the beach was completely empty, and took a long walk along the shoreline with a cup of coffee in my hand. After breakfast, which was usually fresh papaya, mango and warm bread from the village bakery, I grabbed my snorkel and headed into the water. The reef was only a short swim from the beach, and within minutes I was surrounded by colorful fish, slow-moving turtles and the occasional curious reef shark. Every dive felt like entering a different world.
